## The defect

An entry written twice before its first dispatch has two pending upsert ops. At dispatch
`outboxEntryForRow` (`apps/mobile/src/data/sync/store.sqlite.ts`, around line 411) builds the wire
meal of EACH op from the current mirror row, so both carry the same meal and the same
`updatedAt`. The API applies the first (an upsert wins when its `updatedAt` is greater than the
stored one, `wins()` in `apps/api/src/sync.ts`) and answers the second as a loser (not greater than
what it just stored). In `applyAcknowledgedRecord`
(`apps/mobile/src/data/repos/syncMergeRepository.ts`, around line 144) the loser deletes its op and
calls `applyPulledRow` with `force: true`; the pulled record is written into the mirror, and its
detail row is rebuilt by `detailForMeal` (`store.sqlite.ts`, around line 92): `grams`,
`confidence`, `scanId` become null, `source` becomes `'sync'`. The text and the macros are right,
but the entry can no longer recompute its kilocalories from its grams, and it lost the confidence
the scan gave it.

When it happens: any two writes of one entry before the dispatch that carries the first (inside
the debounce of 500 ms, or the whole time the device is offline, or a retry backlog), for example a
meal logged and then edited, an entry edited twice, a save followed by the change of its meal.

## What to build

1. **Tests first, through the engine against the fake server, red on the code of today** (quote the
   red run):
   - an entry edited twice before its first dispatch: after the dispatch the entry shows the second
     edit and keeps its grams, its confidence, its source and its scan id;
   - an entry logged, then edited, while "offline" (the transport fails), then a dispatch that
     succeeds: the same assertions;
   - an entry edited, then deleted, before the dispatch: the server holds the tombstone and the
     local row is gone, with no op left and no error;
   - an entry deleted, then logged again with the same id (the undo of PR 37 does this): the server
     holds the entry and the local row is the one that was written last.
2. **The fix, at the place the design says.** Two candidates; choose one, say why in the findings,
   and reject the other with its reason:
   - at dispatch: a record contributes only its newest pending op to a batch, and the older pending
     ops of that record are removed as superseded in the same read (one op per record per batch);
   - at the acknowledgement: a losing op whose record was accepted in the same batch (the answer
     carries a sibling op id, or the same `updatedAt`) is not a loss against another device: its op
     goes, and the mirror and the detail rows stay as the local write left them.
   A third way, changing what the server answers, is out of scope: the API is not touched.
3. **The merge keeps its contract**: a pulled row never overwrites a row that has a pending local
   intent; a losing op against another device still mirrors the server's winner (the reason the
   `force` path exists). Both get a test that stays green.
4. No change to the wire contract (`packages/api-contract`), to the engine's numbers (batch 50,
   debounce 500 ms, quarantine at 5 attempts), to the schema, or to a migration.
